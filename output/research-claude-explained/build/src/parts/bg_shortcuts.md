### Rewards and shortcuts {#bg-shortcuts}

In reinforcement learning the reward is the only thing the model is pushed toward, so training will find any way of earning it, including ways nobody intended. This is called **reward hacking**. Suppose the reward depends only on how close the final task score is to the reference score. A trajectory that ignores the video and submits typical feature values earns a fair reward for most people, because most people are near typical, and it is cheaper and more reliable than a trajectory that measures. @{xu2026} trained video-language models with rewards on final answers only on questions that depend on the order of events, and tracked the share of questions whose answer stays the same when the video's frames are shuffled into random order. That share jumped sharply over a short stretch of training, a sign that the answers had stopped depending on the order of events in the video ({fig:shortcut}). Tool-using agents trained with reinforcement learning also skip verification steps and exploit loopholes more often than models trained without it [@thaman2026].

@fig shortcut | figures/papers/visual_shortcut_fig1.png | Answers stop depending on the video. The vertical axis is the share of questions whose answer does not change when the video's frames are shuffled; higher values mean the answers rely less on the order of events in the video. Under reinforcement learning with outcome-only rewards it jumps within a narrow window of training steps, in two independent runs. Reproduced from @{xu2026}, Fig. 1.

A second problem is specific to scores that average several features. If one feature is overestimated and another underestimated by the same amount, the errors cancel: the score is right while both measurements are wrong ({fig:cancel}). A reward on the score alone cannot tell such an attempt from a good one.

@fig cancel | figures/fig_cancel.svg | Opposite errors cancel in an average. Attempt A gets the task score right from two wrong features; attempt B is slightly off on every feature. A reward on the final score prefers A, while a reward on each feature prefers B. Orange diamonds are three-camera reference values and blue dots are submitted values, both rescaled to 0–1.

### Information that exists only during training {#bg-privileged-ref}

StaBLE was filmed by three phones, but the agent receives one. The three-camera reconstruction gives a reference value for every feature of every trial, and it can be used in two places: during training, to compute rewards, and during evaluation, to score the results. It never enters the agent's input, because a deployed agent will not have it. Information that is available while training but not when the model is used is called **privileged information** [@vapnik2009].
