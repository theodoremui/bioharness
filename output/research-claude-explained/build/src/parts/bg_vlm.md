### Neural networks, language models and vision-language models {#bg-vlm}

A **neural network** is a mathematical function with a very large number of adjustable numbers, called **parameters** or **weights**. Given an input, such as the pixels of an image, it multiplies and adds those numbers in many layers and produces an output, such as the probability that the image shows a cat. **Training** means adjusting the weights a little at a time so that the outputs on many examples move closer to the desired answers. The model used here has 8 billion weights.

A **language model** is a neural network trained to continue text. Text is cut into small pieces called **tokens**, roughly words or parts of words. Given the tokens so far, the model outputs a probability for every possible next token; choosing one, appending it and repeating produces sentences, code or answers. A **vision-language model (VLM)** adds a second network, the vision encoder, which converts each image or video frame into a sequence of tokens that the language model processes alongside the text ({fig:qwen}). We use Qwen3-VL-8B [@bai2025], an openly released VLM whose weights the lab can download and fine-tune, and which accepts video frames together with their timestamps.
